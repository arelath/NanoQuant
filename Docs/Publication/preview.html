Abstract

Low-rank binary factorization makes post-training compression below one bit per weight possible, but at this precision matrix-local reconstruction error is a weak proxy for language-model behavior. We introduce FunctionalNanoQuant, a set of storage-neutral and exact-budget extensions to NanoQuant. First, an exact-unit functional profiler measures the predictive divergence caused by each physical factorization unit, while a rank-response model allocates aligned rank quanta by predicted functional error reduction per physical bit. Second, operator-coupled scale refitting optimizes the gated MLP as a composed function rather than as three independent matrices, and covariantly folds every learned multiplier into the existing factor scales and correction terms. Third, a top-\(k\)-plus-tail distillation objective removes the confidence invariance of conditional top-\(k\) distillation while retaining a bounded teacher cache. We additionally study shared-input factorization, covariance-aware binary refinement, and a mixed free/codebook factor representation.

On Gemma 3 1B, functional rank allocation reduces WikiText-2 perplexity from 274.9 to 228.6 at essentially unchanged effective bits per weight (BPW). Foldable MLP refitting further reduces protocol-matched perplexity by 5.39% with identical packed bytes and BPW. On a matched frozen factorization, tail-aware distillation reduces WikiText-2 perplexity from 197.4 to 184.2 and improves held-out C4 negative log-likelihood and full-vocabulary teacher KL without an established six-task regression. Introduction

Post-training quantization (PTQ) normally reduces the precision of a dense weight tensor in place. This approach becomes structurally awkward below one bit per source parameter. NanoQuant instead represents a dense matrix by two low-rank binary factors and a small set of floating-point scales, initializes the factors with a latent-binary ADMM procedure, refines them inside a block-reconstruction loop, and finally tunes model-level scales by knowledge distillation (Chong et al. 2026). The representation establishes a strong sub-1-bit size–quality frontier without the data budget of low-rank binary quantization-aware training (Lee et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2024).

Our starting observation is that a successful representation does not make its local reconstruction objective an adequate global decision rule. In the project’s controlled experiments, lower unweighted matrix error often corresponds to worse teacher KL or perplexity; individually favorable changes frequently fail when composed across depth. At approximately one BPW, there is too little redundant capacity to ignore this mismatch.

We therefore ask a narrower question than inventing another binary factorizer: how should a fixed low-rank binary representation spend and recover its capacity when end-model behavior, exact physical bits, and runtime fidelity are all first-class constraints?

FunctionalNanoQuant answers with three mechanisms:

  1. Functional error budgeting. We splice one physical factorization unit at a time into a dense teacher, measure causal full-vocabulary KL, probe the unit’s reconstruction response at several ranks, and allocate exact aligned rank quanta by diminishing predicted gain per bit.

  2. Foldable composed-operator recovery. We fit positive channel multipliers at the gate/up product and downstream projection of a gated MLP, optionally continue them against model-level loss, and fold them into already stored scales and side terms. The serialized model adds no tensor shapes, bytes, or inference operation.

  3. Tail-aware bounded distillation. We cache the teacher’s top-\(k\) logits and one full-vocabulary log-normalizer per selected token. The resulting \(k+1\)-category objective retains the teacher’s aggregate tail mass and removes a confidence-null direction in conditional top-\(k\) KD.

The paper also records algorithms that reveal the limits of local proxies: shared-input and reciprocal factor grouping, dense-covariance sign refinement, sparse-corrected sign codebooks, and a mixed free/coded basis. Some are useful representations or diagnostics but do not yet satisfy full-model transfer. Reporting those negative and partial results prevents a locally attractive ablation from being mistaken for a deployable method.

Our contributions are:

  • an exact-bit, KL-calibrated rank allocator driven by current-run physical-unit measurements and piecewise rank-response curves;

  • an operator-coupled scale-recovery family whose learned parameters are exactly foldable into low-rank binary model components;

  • a bounded-memory top-\(k\)-plus-tail model-reconstruction objective and a diagnosis of conditional top-\(k\) confidence collapse;

  • a mixed free/coded sign-word factor representation that trades coding cost for additional rank at the same physical bit budget; and

  • a reproducible evaluation methodology spanning immutable calibration, paired held-out comparisons, exact BPW, packed execution, resume, and GGUF export.

Background and Problem Formulation

Low-rank binary PTQ

For a dense linear weight \(W\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\), we use the deployed factor form \[\widehat W = \operatorname{diag}(s_{\mathrm{post}}) B_L \operatorname{diag}(s_{\mathrm{mid}}) B_R \operatorname{diag}(s_{\mathrm{pre}}). \tag{1}\] where \(B_L\in\{-1,+1\}^{m\times r}\) and \(B_R\in\{-1,+1\}^{r\times n}\). Optional salient columns, biases, or low-rank side corrections are accounted separately and must be transformed covariantly whenever a boundary scale changes. This form generalizes the two channel-scale formulation in NanoQuant while retaining packed binary execution.

The binary factor cost is rank dependent. Let \(C_i(r)\) be the exact physical cost of unit \(i\) at rank \(r\), including aligned sign words, rank-shaped scales, and fixed side data. Given target budget \(C_{\max}\), the planner must satisfy \[\sum_i C_i(r_i) \le C_{\max} \tag{2}\] without treating alignment residue or metadata as free.

Why local reconstruction is insufficient

Let \(M\) be the dense teacher and \(\widehat M_S\) a model in which only units in \(S\) are replaced by their compressed reconstructions. For token inventory \(\mathcal D\), define the splice divergence \[K(S)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal T|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T} D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(p_M(\cdot\mid x_{<t})\,\|\, p_{\widehat M_S}(\cdot\mid x_{<t})\right). \tag{3}\] The exact-unit profile uses \(K(\{i\})\). This is more expensive than Frobenius error, but for billion-parameter models a complete 130-unit profile is still small relative to factorization and tuning. More importantly, it measures the quantity the allocator is intended to protect.

Functional Error Budgeting

Immutable exact-unit profiling

The profiler begins from a complete baseline factorization. It loads the dense teacher once, caches or streams teacher log-probabilities, and evaluates a factorized splice for each physical unit. A shared QKV group is one arm, not three logical arms. Every profile is bound to the model revision, calibration state, factorization recipe, source tensor hashes, token hashes, and packed-unit topology. Per-sequence NLL and KL are retained for paired bootstrap intervals.

For each unit \(i\), the profile records baseline rank \(r_{i,0}\), exact cost, functional anchor \(K_i\), and calibration-weighted squared reconstruction error \(D_{i,0}\). Earlier variants used \(K_i/D_{i,0}\) as a sensitivity proxy; the accepted exact-unit policy uses \(K_i\) directly and reserves reconstruction error for predicting the effect of a rank change.

Measured rank-response curves

Each unit is probed at aligned lower, baseline, and upper ranks using the current run’s objective and factorization settings. Over the calibrated rank interval, relative reconstruction error is modeled as piecewise exponential. If \(B_i(r)\) is the integrated per-rank slope from \(r_{i,0}\) to \(r\), then \[D_i(r)=D_{i,0}\exp[-2B_i(r)]. \tag{4}\] For a segment with slope \(\beta_{ij}\), \(B_i(r)\) accumulates \(\beta_{ij}\) times the signed rank distance traversed in that segment. The factor two converts the empirical relative-error response to squared error.

Noisy probe intervals that fail to establish improvement are assigned zero marginal gain. Response curves are invalidated if topology, source weights, calibration, ADMM settings, or baseline rank changes.

Exact-bit allocation

At current rank \(r\), one aligned quantum \(\Delta\) receives priority \[\pi_i(r,\Delta)= \frac{a_i\left[D_i(r)-D_i(r+\Delta)\right]} {C_i(r+\Delta)-C_i(r)}, \qquad a_i=\exp\!\left(\gamma(\log K_i-\overline{\log K})\right). \tag{5}\] where \(\overline{\log K}\) is a baseline-cost-weighted geometric mean and \(\gamma\in[0,1]\) tempers sensitivity. The KL-calibrated configuration uses \(\gamma=1\). Starting from legal floors, the deterministic planner repeatedly awards the affordable quantum with maximum \(\pi_i\), recomputing diminishing gain after each award. Architecture-critical units may have explicit floors; all units respect calibrated response bounds and physical rank caps.

Reconstruction-aware precursor

The reconstruction-only precursor uses Eq. (4) with activation/Fisher sensitivity rather than exact-unit KL. It reduced aggregate matrix error in probes but could starve architecture-critical transforms. A complete Gemma 3 270M run reached perplexity 2018.5, substantially worse than the accepted baseline. This negative result motivated exact functional anchors and protected floors rather than invalidating measured rank response itself.

Topology and Objective Variants

Shared-input stacking

For projections \(W_1,\ldots,W_g\) consuming the same input \(x\), row stacking forms \[W_G=\begin{bmatrix}W_1\\ \vdots\\ W_g\end{bmatrix} \approx B_{L,G}\operatorname{diag}(s_G)B_{R,G}. \tag{6}\] so all members share the right/input binary basis. The combined rank is funded by the exact sum of the separate member budgets. Output importance is concatenated in member order; input importance is shared. Outliers and commits belong to the physical group, while logical outputs are row slices.

QKV stacking reduced fixed-budget reconstruction error in preliminary studies, but a complete Gemma 3 270M run regressed both perplexity and task accuracy. We therefore treat shared-input topology as a planner option, not a universal architecture rule. Reciprocal groupings such as \([Q;V]\) with \([K;O^\top]\) also improved matrix objectives but failed held-out attention-function gates.

Fisher weighting and covariance-aware refinement

For reconstruction error \(E=W-\widehat W\), the covariance-aware objective is \[\mathcal J(E)= \operatorname{tr}\!\left(D_{\mathrm{out}}E\Sigma_{\mathrm{in}}E^\top\right). \tag{7}\] where \(D_{\mathrm{out}}\) is diagonal output importance and \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{in}}\) is a dense input covariance. Starting from ordinary ADMM factors, the bounded refinement alternates:

  1. closed-form pre/mid/post scale fits under Eq. (7);

  2. exact left-sign coordinate flips, independent by output row;

  3. right-sign batches whose complete coupled quadratic delta is negative;

  4. final scale fits with rollback if the objective does not improve.

The representation, rank, and persisted bit count do not change. On selected blocks, the 32-step variant reduced held-out covariance error energy by 30.94% and joint-splice KL by 10.47%. However, a later complete production transfer regressed both pre- and post-KD perplexity because the covariance source and placement rule did not transfer. We report covariance refinement as a useful bounded solver and negative general-selection result, not as part of the final default method.

Mixed Free/Coded Binary Factors

Ordinary binary factors store each sign independently. A codebook-constrained factor instead represents each 32-bit sign word by a short codeword index. At equal storage this funds a larger rank, but coding both factors restricts both the basis patterns and their per-output mixtures. We leave \(B_L\) fully free and use a heterogeneous basis for rows of \(B_R\): \[B_R=\begin{bmatrix}B_R^{\mathrm{free}}\\ \operatorname{decode}(z_1,c_1)\\ \vdots\\ \operatorname{decode}(z_q,c_q) \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \operatorname{decode}(z,c)=\mathcal C[z]\oplus \operatorname{mask}(c). \tag{8}\] Here \(\mathcal C\) is a \(2^{10}\)-entry table of 32-bit sign words. Each coded word stores a 10-bit table index and a 9-bit identifier for an unordered pair of corrected bit positions, for a 19-bit record. Component permutation lets the free rows form a prefix, so only one free-row count is required.

The accepted down-projection candidate raises rank from 970 to 1344, retains 256 fully free right-factor rows, and codes the remaining 1088 rows. It uses 7,966,224 bits versus 7,966,624 for the baseline. Across blocks 0, 12, and 24, held-out joint splice KL fell by 8.52%; a later selective five-block policy passed three factorization seeds. The compact payload is expanded once to the canonical packed sign words at load time, leaving the timed linear kernel unchanged.

Operator-Coupled Scale Recovery and Folding

Gated-MLP objective

For gate and up projections, the dense teacher intermediate is \[h^\star=\operatorname{SiLU}(g^\star)\odot u^\star. \tag{9}\] Independent matrix reconstruction cannot exploit cancellation or compensate the nonlinear gate. We fit positive channel multipliers \(a,b\) by minimizing \[\min_{a,b>0}\left\| \operatorname{SiLU}(\widehat g\odot a)\odot(\widehat u\odot b)-h^\star \right\|_F^2. \tag{10}\] within bounded multiplicative limits. The downstream input multiplier \(c\) is then fit against the teacher MLP output: \[\min_{c>0}\left\|y^\star-\widehat W_{\mathrm{down}} (\widehat h\odot c)\right\|_F^2. \tag{11}\] Output-channel fits have closed-form least-squares solutions with identity fallback per channel. Down-input fitting uses diagonal-preconditioned descent and backtracking, accepting only strict objective decreases.

Local selection did not compose reliably. The accepted policy therefore uses fresh language-functional windows to select a small post-KD block mapping and then confirms the complete mapping on untouched data. On the retained Gemma candidate, five selected blocks reduced protocol-matched perplexity from 228.55 to 216.24 (5.39%) while improving five of six limited tasks.

Global foldable continuation

The global variant installs log-multipliers on every MLP factorized linear: gate output, up output, down input, and down output. If \(\theta\) denotes all log-multipliers, training minimizes a held-out reconstruction or distillation loss plus a family-balanced identity penalty \[\mathcal L(\theta)=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{model}}(\theta) +\lambda_{\mathrm{id}}\frac{1}{F}\sum_{f=1}^F \frac{1}{|\theta_f|}\|\theta_f\|_2^2. \tag{12}\] with \(\exp(\theta)\) bounded around one. Optimization uses the exact forward that will result after folding, including salient outliers and optional patch terms.

For Eq. (1), an input multiplier is absorbed into \(s_{\mathrm{pre}}\) and an output multiplier into \(s_{\mathrm{post}}\). Outlier values and low-rank patch terms are rescaled on the corresponding boundary so the complete linear function is preserved. After optimization, the wrapper modules are removed and the rescaled components replace the same number of bytes. Deployment-faithful replay requires zero maximum absolute difference between the unfolded optimization forward and folded model.

Final-normalizer confidence fold

For Gemma RMSNorm, the effective final-normalizer weight is \(1+w\). A scalar \(\alpha\) can therefore be folded exactly as \[w' = \alpha(1+w)-1. \tag{13}\] This changes no shape, byte count, or inference operation. We use it only as explicit calibration, not as evidence of model capability. A fixed \(\alpha=1.015\) passed one retained campaign but failed to transfer to a fresh factorization; the general policy must derive \(\alpha\) on calibration-only data with a predeclared margin or omit the fold when the primary objective is already calibrated.

Tail-Aware Bounded Model Reconstruction

Confidence invariance of conditional top-\(k\) KD

Conditional top-\(k\) distillation normalizes teacher and student logits only within the teacher-selected set \(S_k\). Adding a constant to all selected student logits relative to the unobserved vocabulary tail leaves this loss unchanged. In our long-horizon runs, this null direction caused selected probability mass to collapse even while conditional loss improved.

For teacher probabilities \(p_i\), student probabilities \(q_i\), selected masses \(M_p=\sum_{i\in S_k}p_i\) and \(M_q=\sum_{i\in S_k}q_i\), define \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal L_{\mathrm{shape}} &= -\sum_{i\in S_k}p_i\log\frac{q_i}{M_q},\\ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{mass}} &= -M_p\log M_q -(1-M_p)\log(1-M_q),\\ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{tail}} &= \mathcal L_{\mathrm{shape}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{tail}} \mathcal L_{\mathrm{mass}}. \end{aligned} \tag{14}\] When \(\lambda_{\mathrm{tail}}=1\), Eq. (14) is exactly the cross entropy over the teacher’s \(k\) selected categories plus one aggregated tail category. Other positive values trade conditional shape against calibration.

The teacher cache stores selected indices, selected logits, and one full-vocabulary log-normalizer per selected token. Vocabulary and token chunking bound memory without approximating the target. On the matched Gemma factorization, eight epochs capped at 32 batches (256 optimizer steps) and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{tail}}=0.5\) outperformed both 2048-step conditional KD and 256-step conditional KD on fresh WikiText and C4 NLL/KL.

One-sided mass-floor correction

We also evaluated a short warm-start correction that preserves conditional shape while applying pressure only below a target selected mass. With \(\rho\in(0,1]\) and batch means \(\bar M_p,\bar M_q\), \[\mathcal L_{\mathrm{floor}}= \mathcal L_{\mathrm{conditional}}+\mu\, \operatorname{ReLU}\!\left[ \operatorname{logit}(\rho\bar M_p)-\operatorname{logit}(\bar M_q) \right]. \tag{15}\] The correction exactly vanishes once the batch constraint is met. A low- pressure 32-step correction plus a small final-norm fold improved retained WikiText and C4 results at unchanged BPW, but the fixed recipe missed its mass floor on a fresh factorization. It is therefore an ablation and fallback, not the preferred primary objective. The preferred method attacks confidence drift inside the primary tail-aware objective and uses selected mass as a calibration diagnostic rather than a capability gate.

Systems Realization

Each scientific state is content addressed and bound to semantic inputs: model revision, calibration tokens and statistics, topology, objective, rank plan, factorizer version, and tuning protocol. Block and layer commits are immutable. Rolling activation generations bound disk use while completed factor results remain durable. Epoch checkpoints make factorized and global tuning resume-equivalent across process boundaries.

The deployment path validates logical factors, packs sign words, creates a hash-bound converter checkpoint, exports GGUF, and evaluates both a PyTorch reference and the modified llama.cpp path. Exact BPW includes binary factors, scales, outliers, corrections, alignment, and declared metadata. No algorithm is called storage neutral merely because its primary tensor shapes are unchanged.

The mixed-basis overlay is deliberately separate from the canonical packed layout. Selected compact factors predecode once at model load to the ordinary packed words; unselected layers fall back lazily to the base artifact. This keeps the hot kernel unchanged and makes representation experiments reversible.

Related Work

Binary and sub-1-bit LLMs.

OneBit, LittleBit, DBF, BiLLM, and related methods explore binary weights, low-rank binary factors, QAT, and binary PTQ (Xu et al. 2024; Lee et al. 2025; Boza and Macko 2026; Huang et al. 2024). NanoQuant combines low-rank binary factors with Hessian-aware ADMM initialization, block reconstruction, model-level scale tuning, and binary CUDA kernels (Chong et al. 2026). Our work retains its factorization primitive and focuses on functional allocation, foldable recovery, and bounded tail-aware calibration.

Quantization-aware reconstruction and allocation.

GPTQ, AWQ, OmniQuant, and low-rank error-reconstruction methods use curvature, activation statistics, learned clipping/scaling, or additive low-rank terms to preserve quantized models (Frantar et al. 2023; Lin et al. 2023; Shao et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2024). Our allocator differs by measuring the end-model KL contribution of each current physical factorization unit, joining it to current-run rank-response probes, and optimizing exact rank-dependent physical cost.

Scale reparameterization.

SmoothQuant migrates quantization difficulty through an equivalent channel transformation, while RepQuant decouples expressive quantization-time scales from hardware-friendly inference scales (Xiao et al. 2023; Li et al. 2024). Our folding algebra belongs to this broad family. The new element is the composed gated-MLP and model-functional optimization of existing low-rank binary boundary scales, with covariant treatment of salient and patch terms and byte-identical serialization.

Sparse logit distillation.

Sparse teacher caches reduce the cost of logit KD, but naive top-\(k\) targets are biased and hide the vocabulary tail (Anshumann et al. 2025). Random-sampling estimators preserve the full gradient in expectation, while other methods emphasize selected logit ranking or scaling (Wang and Zhou 2025). Our objective is not an unbiased estimator of full-vocabulary KL. It is an exact coarse-grained \(k+1\)-category cross entropy with an adjustable conditional/mass tradeoff, chosen because model reconstruction updates few stored scale parameters under a strict memory budget.

Limitations and Responsible Interpretation

The strongest current evidence is concentrated on Gemma 3 1B. Gemma-specific rank sensitivities, selected blocks, tail coefficient, and final-normalizer fold must not be advertised as universal. The project has an operational Llama 3.2 1B result, but not yet protocol-matched transfer of the complete new method; larger Llama and Qwen campaigns remain incomplete or planned.

Functional profiling consumes teacher inference and can overfit its token inventory if slice lifecycle is not enforced. Greedy rank allocation assumes the measured marginal response is sufficiently separable; cross-unit interactions remain only indirectly represented by the splice baseline and final gates. Tail-aware distillation trades an exact full-vocabulary target for a coarse tail bucket and is not suitable when tail shape itself is the task signal.

Several locally successful algorithms failed complete transfer. We therefore distinguish matrix, operator, splice, frozen-model, and production/export evidence. Placeholders in this draft are claims that must remain absent from the abstract and conclusion until their gates are complete.

Conclusion

Sub-1-bit post-training compression leaves little room for a poor proxy. We show that a low-rank binary model can spend its exact bit budget using measured functional sensitivity, recover coupled operator behavior through parameters that fold into its existing representation, and preserve confidence with a bounded top-\(k\)-plus-tail target. On the retained Gemma workloads, each accepted component improves a matched baseline without increasing effective storage. Negative-Result Details

Topology is not selected by matrix error.

The fixed QV/KO attention partition improved the corrected-Fisher matrix objective by approximately 1.04% across two seeds but increased held-out full-attention teacher KL by 27.29%. Per-block matrix-oracle selection is therefore not a valid topology policy without an operator-functional selector.

Covariance is not a transferable placement score.

Dense-covariance refinement reduced its optimization objective at every selected production placement, yet the fresh model regressed from 273.52 to 286.08 pre-KD perplexity and from 228.55 to 241.12 post-KD. The offline and resident covariance sources differed, and local covariance reduction did not predict composition across blocks.

Biases and residual patches must pay their rank opportunity cost.

For calibration input mean \(\bar x\), the closed-form output correction \(b=\bar x(W-\widehat W)^\top\) removes the mean activation residual without changing the binary factors. Likewise, a low-rank patch \(P_LP_R\) or sparse residual entries can remove a large fraction of local activation error. However, their exact stored indices, values, and factors displace binary rank under an equal-bit budget. The all-unit bias arm increased an \(o_{\mathrm{proj}}\) splice KL by 21.66%, and a locally strong rank-4 residual patch increased the equal-budget model KL by 14.25%. Sparse entries were consistently more efficient than complete residual columns, but did not recover enough of the rank-loss penalty for promotion. These remain supported sidecar mechanisms, not default quality improvements.

Group member weighting can be real but non-additive.

Reweighting value-projection rows inside a stacked QKV objective produced a large preliminary splice improvement, but the first sweep regenerated nonidentical Fisher inputs. A corrected combined “best methods” experiment that included stacked QKV and a doubled value objective did not beat the simpler exact-unit D2 recipe. Member weighting is therefore a controlled objective parameter requiring identical calibration artifacts, not an accepted universal multiplier.

Untuned overlays do not replace resident tuning.

A complete MLP hybrid improved 29.68% relative to its equal-budget untuned sign-word family, but direct installation into the retained frozen model raised perplexity from 273.87 to 1904.75. The relative representation result remains valid; the absolute transfer failed because the overlay bypassed block-local factor tuning, non-factorized tuning, outliers, and post-block refit.

A fixed confidence fold does not transfer.

The retained low-pressure correction plus \(\alpha=1.015\) final-norm fold passed all local gates, but on a fresh factorization selected mass was 0.7305, below the predeclared 0.75 floor. This motivated the primary tail-aware objective and the treatment of scalar confidence as calibration rather than capability.
